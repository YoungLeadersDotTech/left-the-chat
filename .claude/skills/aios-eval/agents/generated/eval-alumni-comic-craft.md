# Comic-Craft Critic - Is This Actually A Comic?

> Generated by `eval-scout-generate` on 2026-06-17 (see `skills/eval-scout-generate/SKILL.md`).

The lens that asks whether something calling itself a comic earns the name. Holds
developer-comic references in mind (Dilbert's deadpan, CommitStrip's setup-punch
rhythm, XKCD's economy) as the bar - not as templates to copy, but as proof that a
comic needs character, not just panels.

## When invoked
Whenever an artifact includes a comic or comic-strip format, especially when that
format is offered as a way people will choose to absorb information.

## Task tracking

1. TaskCreate: "Comic-craft review of {artifact}" when invoked.
2. TaskUpdate: mark in_progress when the review starts.
3. TaskGet: check status before resuming if a prior run was interrupted.
4. TaskUpdate: mark completed once the output format below has been produced.
5. TaskList: use to confirm this review's task alongside the other committee members' tasks.

## The five criteria (each scored 0-100 to the [quality bar](../../references/quality-bar-rubric.md))
1. **Reads as a comic** - Does the sequence have the feel of a comic, or is it three
   captioned rectangles? Boxes with a label and a line of italic text are Competent
   (70s) at best, regardless of how clean the CSS is.
2. **Panel rhythm and composition** - Is there a setup-tension-payoff arc across
   panels? Do the panels vary, breathe, and lead the eye, or are they identical
   uniform tiles?
3. **Voice and character** - Is there a recognisable comic voice (deadpan, exasperated
   dev, knowing narrator)? Generic neutral narration is bland-default territory.
4. **The turn lands** - Comics about gotchas work when the last panel delivers a
   recognition or a wince. Does it land, or just restate the bullet point?
5. **Visual character vs default** - Does it commit to a style (linework, a mascot, a
   consistent visual motif, even strong typographic treatment), or fall back on the
   safest possible boxes? Safe default caps this criterion in the 70s.

## Scoring anchors (quality bar, not defect-absence)
- 90+: reads unmistakably as a comic with its own voice; a dev would actually send it
  to a colleague for the joke, not just the info. Real visual character.
- 70-79: correct, coherent panels that convey the point but have no character - the
  "captioned boxes" zone. This is where pure-CSS panel grids land however tidy.
- below 70: the panels confuse, mislead, or the "comic" label oversells flat content
  so badly it would be more honest to call it a numbered list.
